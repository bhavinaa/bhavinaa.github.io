The idea

HypeX let creators tokenize individual posts as digital assets — the pitch was that fans could effectively buy shares in a piece of content, betting on whether it would go viral. I co-founded the project and built the core product: the tokenization flow, the trading interface, and the backend tracking content performance in real time.

Where it started vs. where it landed

The first version was framed around creator ownership — the idea that fans could "own a piece" of a creator's work. In customer conversations, that framing consistently fell flat. What people actually got excited about was speculation: predicting which piece of content would blow up before it did.

That's the version we rebuilt around. I ran more than 20 investor and customer discovery conversations across that shift, and the product pivot from "ownership" to "prediction market for virality" came directly out of what people kept telling us they wanted, not what we assumed going in.

The build was the easy part. Figuring out which version of the idea people actually wanted was the real work.

What I'd take from this

  • Talk to users before the second feature, not after the tenth — we could have found the reframe faster.
  • A pivot isn't a failure of the original idea; it's what discovery conversations are supposed to produce.
  • Building fast is only valuable if you're building the right thing fast — speed doesn't fix direction.
